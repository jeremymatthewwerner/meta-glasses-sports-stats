# Nobody yet turns Meta glasses into scorebooks

No shipping product, open or closed, turns Meta smart-glasses footage into team-sport stats, play logs or player galleries. The space is open, but it is open because the hard parts are still hard. The incumbents (Hudl, Veo, Pixellot, Trace, GameChanger) all assume a fixed, elevated camera or a human scorer. The only sports partner on Meta's developer toolkit is a golf app. Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit (DAT) reached a stable 1.0 in late September 2026. Even so, a third-party app gets at most a 720x1280, 30fps stream over Bluetooth to the phone, and the glasses manage only about 30-50 minutes of total recording per charge. That is not enough to film a 1-3 hour game continuously. A permissive open-source vision stack (RF-DETR, ByteTrack, Torchreid, PARSeq, Whisper, Qwen3-VL) plus mature open data schemas (nflfastR, SPADL/kloppy, Retrosheet) covers most of the pipeline. Soccer has the deepest tooling and American football the thinnest, which runs opposite to the planned launch order. The binding constraints are legal and policy, not technical. Meta's own acceptable-use policy bans identifying people by face or voice unless Meta grants a prior-approval exception. Face templates, especially of minors, trigger BIPA, Colorado, Texas, the amended COPPA rule and GDPR Art. 9. Little League claims ownership of game video. Players wearing consumer smart glasses in games likely breaks equipment rules. The viable product has five parts. Jersey number plus team color is the primary, lower-risk way to identify players. Face recognition is a supported, high-risk, opt-in second path for pickup games without jerseys, limited to people a teammate, parent or coach has enrolled with consent. Voice-assisted logging by a sideline parent or coach supplies the stats, libraries stay team-private, and player POV is for practice only. Veo recordings belong in scope only as a secondary input for soccer: a user-uploaded follow-cam MP4 fused with glasses footage. They do not justify an API integration.

## No shipping product turns glasses footage into team stats

The closed-source market splits three ways: humans who tag video, AI cameras that film from a fixed point, and phone apps that score by hand. None of them treats a wearer's POV as a first-class input.

**Football.** Hudl dominates youth and high school film. Its Assist breakdown is done by "professional human analysts", and its FAQ says AI is used only for volleyball ([Hudl Assist FAQ](https://www.hudl.com/products/assist/faq)). Player identity in Assist comes from metadata the submitter enters: jersey colors, who played, the final score. Club football teams pay **$450 to $1,700 per team per season** for the service ([Hudl pricing](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/assist/football)). QwikCut, the main challenger, also uses human taggers and quotes a turnaround of "as little as 12 hours" ([QwikCut](https://qwikcut.com/football/)). Two AI startups say they automate the work. BallHawk claims plays, formations and tendencies are tagged "in minutes" and exports to Hudl and CSV ([BallHawk](https://ballhawkfootball.com/)). PlayIQ claims 22-player tracking but recommends a high sideline camera ([PlayIQ](https://theplayiq.com/ai-football-film-analysis/)). Neither publishes accuracy figures. Every football workflow assumes elevated all-22 film, which a sideline POV cannot replace.

**Soccer.** Soccer is the most automated of the three sports. Veo, Pixellot, Spiideo, XbotGo and Hudl Focus film unattended. Veo Analytics 2, launched 19 August 2026, attributes "every shot, pass, and tackle" to a player through shirt-number detection, with no wearables and no manual tagging ([Veo update](https://updates.veo.co/announcements/ann_93FN3qsJTPLfd); [Veo Analytics](https://www.veo.com/en-us/product/veo-analytics)). Trace solves identity a different way: each player wears a BLE beacon, and the service costs $180-$300 a year ([Trace](https://traceup.com/academy/best-ai-sports-cameras-for-automatically-recording-youth-games-in-2026)).

**Baseball.** Youth baseball still runs on a parent scoring by hand. GameChanger only cuts a clip after a play is logged, "so scoring in real time is essential" ([GameChanger Help](https://help.gc.com/hc/en-us/articles/4482237253517-Automatic-Video-Clips-for-Baseball-Softball)). The research found no commercial product that auto-scores a youth baseball game from video.

| Sport | Dominant workflow | Who identifies players | Automation status |
|---|---|---|---|
| American football | Hudl or QwikCut film plus human breakdown | Human, from a roster the submitter supplies | AI startups make claims; no published accuracy |
| Soccer | AI fixed cameras (Veo, Pixellot, Trace) | Jersey OCR (Veo) or a beacon on each player (Trace) | Events and per-player stats shipped (Veo, Aug 2026) |
| Baseball/softball | GameChanger manual scoring | Scorer enters the lineup | No video auto-scoring found |

The pattern across the market is that AI arrives first where the view is constrained and the action is discrete. Balltime (volleyball) and SportsVisio (basketball) are examples, and Hudl bought Balltime in February 2025 ([Hudl blog](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-expands-volleyball-focus-through-game-changing-acquisition-of-balltime)). Hudl made five acquisitions in 2025 alone ([Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2026/06/hudl-celebrates-20-years-demonstrating-global-growth-and-reach-in-sports-video-analysis/)). That makes acquisition by Hudl the default exit, and export to Hudl the default interoperability target.

On the glasses side, DAT's launch partners include Twitch, Streamlabs, Be My Eyes and one sports app, the golf app 18Birdies. Meta's April 2026 showcase named no sports or fitness apps at all ([Meta dev blog](https://developers.meta.com/blog/introducing-meta-wearables-device-access-toolkit/); [Meta dev blog, Apr 2026](https://developers.meta.com/blog/explore-whats-possible-with-wearables-device-access-toolkit/)). The closest analogs are Simply Draw (time-lapse recording through DAT) and a prototype called Hide n'Seek that records during play with kids. Both show that the record-to-phone pattern is accepted. The demand for POV is proven at the top of the market: the Bundesliga's Riedel-built RefCam headset continues into 2026-27, and testers preferred the head-mounted angle ([SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/04/07/a-new-pov-refcams-rise-in-the-bundesliga-signals-a-potential-new-era-for-soccer-broadcasts/)). Nobody packages that view for amateurs.

## DAT 1.0 caps live analysis at 720p and half an hour of battery

All six target devices are 12MP camera glasses, but they record very different video. Every model tops out at about 3K/30 in portrait orientation. Landscape capture had not shipped as of Connect 2026 ([Engadget Gen 3 hands-on](https://www.engadget.com/2267213/ray-ban-meta-gen-3-hands-on-better-battery-life/)), which is awkward for filming a field. Native clips are capped at about three minutes ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/17/ray-ban-meta-gen-2-battery-video-upgrades/)).

The full-game problem is real. Android Central measured about **50 minutes of total 1080p recording on HSTN and about 30 on Gen 1**. Nobody has published a continuous-recording or streaming test for Gen 3, Vanguard or Display ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/gaming/virtual-reality/oakley-meta-hstn-camera-review)). DAT 1.0 exposes thermal and battery state and renamed its stream errors for "thermal, peak power, battery", which means sessions do get killed ([DAT CHANGELOG](https://github.com/facebook/meta-wearables-dat-ios/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). The cases recharge the glasses to 50% in about 20 minutes ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/17/ray-ban-meta-gen-2-battery-video-upgrades/)), so halftime and between-quarter swaps are workable. Continuous capture for three hours is not.

DAT itself became a "stable, supported release" on 30 September 2026 ([Meta Wearables FAQ](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/faq/)). Some trade-press coverage of the 2025 preview, which said apps could not draw on the display, is now out of date. DAT runs on the phone, in Swift or Kotlin. It streams **720x1280 at most, at 2, 7, 15, 24 or 30 fps**, over a Bluetooth Classic link that lowers resolution and then frame rate when bandwidth is tight ([Meta Android guide](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/docs/develop/dat/build-integration-android/)). Version 0.5 added HEVC streaming that keeps running in the background, and version 0.8 added a Wi-Fi transport whose limits are unpublished. Version 1.0 added experimental modules for photo capture, camera audio, IMU motion, glasses input events, on-device speech and "Hey Meta" app launch ([DAT CHANGELOG](https://github.com/facebook/meta-wearables-dat-ios/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). The README marks standalone photo capture and synchronized audio as **"unavailable for production publishing"** ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebook/meta-wearables-dat-ios)). Distribution is the second bottleneck: submission to Meta's discovery surfaces is still "coming soon", and release channels were limited to 100 testers as of May 2026 ([Connect 2026 recap](https://developers.meta.com/blog/meta-connect-recap/); [Meta dev blog](https://developers.meta.com/blog/build-for-display-glasses/)). Gen 3 is not named in the changelog. Meta says only that "new SKUs are supported as they launch".

**Architecture.** Third-party code never runs on displayless glasses, so all computer vision has to happen on the phone or in the cloud ([Meta Wearables FAQ](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/faq/)). The Display model is the exception: HTML/JS Web Apps run on it directly, and Meta's own example use case is "real-time scores and status updates" ([Meta dev blog](https://developers.meta.com/blog/build-for-display-glasses/)). That makes Display the natural confirmation screen for a scorer: the wearer says "touchdown, 22", and the glasses show the parsed event for approval. Meta AI Connectors, in developer preview, expose a service as MCP tools that can be called inside Meta AI conversations on the glasses. That is a second way to build voice logging ([Connect 2026 recap](https://developers.meta.com/blog/meta-connect-recap/)).

**Capture strategy.** The capture design that follows has three paths. The first is a low-frame-rate or burst DAT stream for live segmentation and voice capture. The second is native 3K three-minute clips imported later through the Meta AI app for highlight quality. The third is an optional fixed phone or Veo camera as the full-game backbone.

**Modes.** In spectator mode, Vanguard's 122-degree lens and stabilization make it the best capture device and Display the best scorer's device. Player mode is a separate problem, covered under the legal constraints below.

## A permissive open-source stack covers soccer best and football worst

The research found no end-to-end open-source project for amateur auto play-by-play in any of the three sports. The components, though, are mature. The main trap is licensing.

**Detection and tracking.** Ultralytics YOLO is the most widely used detector, but it is **AGPL-3.0**, and Ultralytics requires an Enterprise license for any business product, internal tools included ([Ultralytics License](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)). That license question carries over to models trained with it, such as BaseballCV's ball and bat trackers. A fully permissive alternative exists: RF-DETR or RT-DETR (Apache-2.0) for detection, Roboflow `supervision` and `sports` (MIT) for glue code and team clustering, ByteTrack, OC-SORT or BoT-SORT (MIT) for tracking, and Torchreid (MIT) for re-ID ([roboflow/sports](https://github.com/roboflow/sports); [rf-detr](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr); [BoT-SORT](https://github.com/NirAharon/BoT-SORT)). BoT-SORT's camera-motion compensation matters most for head-worn footage. So does the optical-flow-aided Kalman prediction used by a top SoccerNet 2025 entry ([arXiv 2508.19182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19182)). SAM 2 is Apache-2.0. SAM 3 moved to a custom license, which should be read before shipping ([Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/model-licenses/segment-anything-3)).

**Jersey numbers.** Koshkina and Elder's pipeline is the best open option. It runs a legibility filter, crops the torso using pose, reads the number with PARSeq and votes per tracklet. It reaches **87.4% tracklet accuracy on SoccerNet** ([arXiv 2405.13896](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13896v1)). The 2025 SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction winner switched to LLaMA-3.2-Vision to read jersey numbers and roles ([arXiv 2508.19182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19182)). VLM fallbacks are now competitive with specialized OCR. Every open benchmark is soccer or hockey, and none covers helmeted football players or baseball. A team roster shrinks the problem to a closed set of 15-25 numbers, which should help. Head-worn close-ups help further, because the far-touchline numbers that defeat fixed cameras are not the ones a parent's POV captures.

**By sport, the tooling is lopsided.**

*Soccer.* Soccer has SoccerNet: benchmarks for action spotting, game-state reconstruction, calibration, re-ID and jersey numbers. In 2026 it added player-centric ball action spotting, which attaches the jersey number to each action ([arXiv 2607.07320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07320)). Field registration is solved for soccer by PnLCalib and No-Bells-Just-Whistles.

*Football.* American football has rich pro tracking data (Big Data Bowl) and Kaggle helmet datasets, but its formation and play-type research is academic work from 2013-2024 with no maintained code ([CVPRW 2013](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_workshops_2013/W19/papers/Atmosukarto_Automatic_Recognition_of_2013_CVPR_paper.pdf); [WACV 2014](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~sinisa/research/publications/wacv14.pdf)). No maintained yard-line registration repo exists. A USPTO patent on detecting the snap from formation detection is a freedom-to-operate item to review ([USPTO 12315258](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12315258)).

*Baseball.* Baseball has BaseballCV (MIT code, Ultralytics-trained weights) and pybaseball (MIT), but no open auto-scorekeeping project ([BaseballCV](https://github.com/BaseballCV/BaseballCV); [pybaseball](https://github.com/jldbc/pybaseball)).

*Egocentric footage.* Ego-Exo4D contributes 1,422 hours with gaze, IMU and SLAM. Its soccer and basketball content is skill drills, not games, and no egocentric team-game dataset with event labels exists ([Ego-Exo4D](https://docs.ego-exo4d-data.org/)). A builder will have to collect and label their own glasses footage.

**VLMs.** Video-language models change the economics of labeling. A 2026 study split soccer broadcasts into 15-second clips, built a 15-frame storyboard per clip, aligned a Whisper transcript, and prompted GPT-5.5 zero-shot. It scored **F1 72.02 against 38.49 for a cross-dataset supervised NetVLAD++**, though the baseline was not retrained, so the comparison favors the VLM ([arXiv 2610.37583](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37583)). Rule-heavy reasoning is still weak: GPT-4o managed only 57.8% on SPORTU's hardest tier ([SPORTU](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.08474)). Open VLMs such as Qwen3-VL and InternVideo are Apache-2.0 for on-premises use ([Qwen3-VL](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL)).

**Audio.** Speech recognition is solved and permissively licensed: Whisper and faster-whisper (MIT), whisperX (BSD-2, word timestamps) and whisper.cpp for on-device use ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper); [whisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX)). Whistle detection has only RoboCup-scale datasets, so plan to train your own ([bit-bots](https://github.com/bit-bots/whistle_detection)).

**Scoreboards.** Scoreboard OCR is fragile. The ScoreSight layout model read the clock correctly 74% of the time and the period 43% ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/royshilkrot/scoreboard-layout)). Many youth fields have no scoreboard at all.

**Recommended pipeline.** The evidence points to a hybrid. Cheap computer vision segments the game: snap and whistle in football, pitch delivery in baseball, possessions in soccer. A VLM labels each short segment. The wearer's own spoken cues ("ball four", "Jake, seven-yard run") are the highest-precision stats signal, and they cost nothing to capture through the glasses' microphones.

## Retrosheet, SPADL and nflfastR already define the data model

No new stats schema is needed. Each sport already has an open, machine-readable standard, and a product should store raw facts in a sport-neutral core while exporting to these formats.

**Baseball.** Retrosheet's `play` record is essentially a machine-readable scorebook. Its fields are inning, team, batter, count, a pitch string (C, S, F, B, X...), and an event string with fielder sequences, modifiers and runner advances such as `63/G.2-3` ([Retrosheet](https://www.retrosheet.org/eventfile.htm)). The most valuable youth stat is the **pitch count**, because it is a compliance rule, not a nice-to-have. Little League caps pitches by age (50 for ages 6-8, 75 for 9-10, 85 for 11-12, 95 for 13-16) and requires up to four days' rest above 66 pitches ([Little League](https://www.littleleague.org/playing-rules/pitch-count/)).

**Soccer.** SPADL defines 12 attributes and 22 action types, and kloppy loads about 15 vendor formats into one model and exports Sportscode XML ([socceraction](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html); [kloppy](https://kloppy.pysport.org/)). The youth stat set (goals, assists, shots, saves, cards, subs) is a strict subset of it.

**Football.** nflfastR provides one row per play with a small `play_type` enum (pass, run, kickoff, punt, field_goal, extra_point and a few more) and player columns per role ([nflfastR source](https://github.com/nflverse/nflfastR/blob/master/R/helper_add_nflscrapr_mutations.R)). Defensive credit is where rule sets diverge. On a sack that causes a fumble, the NCAA credits the fumble but not the sack, while the NFL credits a zero-yard sack, a tackle for loss and a forced fumble ([Sports Info Solutions](https://www.sportsinfosolutions.com/2023/08/07/when-a-sack-isnt-a-sack-revisited)). High school stats flow into MaxPreps, where state associations pull their leaderboards ([GHSA](https://www.ghsa.net/maxpreps-stats/football)). Entry is patchy: only about a third of Washington's 4A teams entered every game ([HeraldNet](https://www.heraldnet.com/sports/lack-of-centralized-database-leaves-void-for-prep-football-stats)). That burden is the coach's real pain point.

**Core model.** The design that follows from these schemas has five parts. A `Game` record carries a rule set (for example LL-11U, NFHS-FB or FLAG-5v5). Each `Event` carries a sport-typed payload, participants with roles, clock and wall-clock times, `video_refs` (clip id, start, end, source device), a `confidence` score and a `source` of auto, human or human-confirmed. `GameState` (score, down and distance, count and outs) is derived from events, never entered. A `StatRuleSet` computes credits, so corrections recompute every stat. The gallery "sortable by player and event" is a query over `Event.participants` joined to `video_refs`, and the same events feed the generated game summaries. IPTC SportsML 3.0 exists as a multi-sport XML standard, but no youth platform uses it ([IPTC](https://iptc.org/news/iptc-releases-sportsml-3-0-standard/)).

| Auto-detectable from video | Auto plus human confirmation | Needs human input |
|---|---|---|
| Goals, TDs, HRs, score changes, pitch delivered, kickoffs/punts, corners, cards shown | Run vs pass, completions/INTs, yards gained, shots on target, saves, hit vs out, swing/miss | Tackle and assist credit, half sacks, penalty type/player, soccer assists, fouls/offside, hit vs error, earned runs, ball/strike without audio |

The feasibility split above is synthesized from the per-sport schema research ([Retrosheet](https://www.retrosheet.org/eventfile.htm); [socceraction](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)). It argues for treating "proposed" and "confirmed" as first-class event states, not as an afterthought.

What users value is narrower than what is technically possible. NCSA recruiting guidance wants the first frame of a highlight to show name, school, jersey number, position and graduation year. It suggests about 20-25 clips over 3-6 minutes for soccer, with full-game video available behind the reel ([NCSA](https://www.ncsasports.org/blog/tips-creating-great-highlight-video); [NCSA soccer](https://ncsasports.org/mens-soccer/recruiting-video-highlights)). Correctly attributed per-player clips and a basic stat line beat advanced analytics for parents and recruits.

## Face ID, Little League and player-worn glasses set the legal limits

**Face recognition is the highest-risk feature in the product.** Meta's Wearables Acceptable Use Policy (updated 22 September 2026) prohibits using device sensors "for unique identification of people based on physical characteristics, such as voices and faces", except with prior Meta approval for cases such as accessibility apps. It also bans surreptitious capture and anything that obscures the capture LED ([Meta AUP](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/acceptable-use-policy/)). The Developer Terms add bans on re-identification and on selling data, and require deletion on request ([Meta Terms](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/terms/)). Since July 2026, glasses firmware disables the camera if the LED is blocked mid-recording ([GSMArena](https://www.gsmarena.com/metas_smart_glasses_will_disable_recording_when_capture_led_is_blocked-news-73645.php)). The voice clause matters for design. The app can transcribe what the wearer says, but it cannot use voiceprints to work out who is speaking.

**Privacy law points the same way.** Courts have held that face templates derived from photos are BIPA biometric identifiers ([Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2022/04/breaking-federal-judge-in-illinois-affirms-that-bipa-extends-to-information-derived-from-photographs/)). The 2024 amendment keeps statutory damages at **$1,000 per negligent and $5,000 per reckless violation**, now counted per person ([Shook Hardy](https://shb.com/intelligence/client-alerts/pds/may-2024-bipa-amendment-wolfe-searle)). Colorado's biometric rules apply to entities of any size ([BiometricUpdate](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202506/texas-and-colorado-take-diverging-paths-on-biometric-privacy)). Texas CUBI carries penalties up to $25,000 per violation. The amended COPPA rule, enforceable since 22 April 2026, expressly covers facial templates and already treats children's photos and video as personal information ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm); [Infolaw Group](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2025/4/29/little-data-big-requirements-is-your-business-ready-for-coppas-amendments)). Under GDPR, a photo becomes special-category biometric data only when it is processed to identify someone uniquely ([Recital 51](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-51/)). Jersey-number OCR is not biometric. Appearance or gait re-ID is a gray area, so keep any embeddings within a single clip and never store or link them across games.

**Face recognition can still be offered, as a consented, human-seeded second path.** The intended design never recognizes strangers. A teammate, parent or coach labels a few shots of a person and links each face to a roster entry. The roster comes from a friends list or from a team app such as BYGA, TeamSnap or GameChanger. The system then matches only those enrolled people. That matters for pickup games, where nobody wears a number. Enrolled-only matching does not remove any of the legal triggers above, because the enrollment itself creates a face template. It does make each requirement satisfiable.

Meta comes first. The AUP's exception path, "prior Meta approval" for specific use cases, is the only sanctioned route, so the app needs a written exception from Meta before face matching runs on glasses-sensor data. It must also stay clear of the Developer Terms' re-identification ban ([Meta AUP](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/acceptable-use-policy/); [Meta Terms](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/terms/)). Running the matching on footage from another source, such as a phone or a Veo upload, does not obviously escape the policy if glasses footage feeds the same feature. Counsel and Meta developer relations should confirm that.

The statutes add a consent requirement for each one:

| Regime | What face enrollment requires |
|---|---|
| BIPA, CUBI and Colorado | Per-person informed consent before enrollment. BIPA's 2024 amendment confirms consent can be collected by electronic signature ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/breaking-bipa-news-illinois-curtails-massive-damage-awards-in-biometric-cases)). |
| COPPA | Verifiable parental consent before enrolling any child under 13, because facial templates are now named personal information ([Infolaw Group](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2025/4/29/little-data-big-requirements-is-your-business-ready-for-coppas-amendments)). |
| GDPR and UK GDPR | Face matching is Art. 9 special-category processing ([Recital 51](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-51/)). Explicit consent is the realistic lawful basis, with a DPIA for EU and UK users. |

The mitigations follow directly from those requirements. Enrollment is opt-in for each person, and a guardian enrolls each minor. Consent is scoped to one team or group and recorded separately from consent to appear in video. Templates live in a team-scoped store and are never matched across teams or against anyone not enrolled. Unmatched faces stay anonymous rather than being clustered. Withdrawing consent, or a guardian's request, deletes the template and its derived tags. A published retention schedule purges templates when a season or group ends. The app shows a clear "face recognition on" notice at enrollment and in recording.

**League rules can override the camera owner.** Little League claims "the exclusive right to authorize any and all webcasting (live or recorded)". It says game video between chartered teams "remains the property of Little League Baseball" and cannot be shared with teams or platforms without written consent ([Little League FAQs](https://www.littleleague.org/help-center/internet-webcast-and-streaming-faqs/)). Postseason high school events are often NFHS Network exclusives. Colorado's association makes the school pay the rights fee if parents live-stream ([CHSAA](https://chsaanow.com/sports/2025/10/28/chsaa-nfhs-network-postseason-rights.aspx)). SafeSport's MAAPP, which governs U.S. Soccer and other Olympic-movement bodies, gives parents the right to refuse recording of their child without losing participation. It also requires adult-to-minor electronic communication to be "open and transparent", which rules out private coach-to-player clip DMs ([SafeSport MAAPP](https://uscenterforsafesport.org/2025-maapp-manual)).

**The two modes face different equipment and officiating rules.**

*Player mode.* IFAB Law 4 permits only non-dangerous equipment such as "sports spectacles", at the referee's discretion ([The FA, Law 4](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/files/thefaportal/governance-docs/laws-of-the-game/2025-26/law-4-the-players-equipment.ashx)). Consumer glasses with hinges and a battery are unlikely to qualify. No impact certification for Meta or Oakley frames was found. A device under a football helmet adds injury risk. In-game first-person capture is effectively out of scope. Player mode should target practices, drills and informal play, where Ego-Exo4D-style skill feedback fits, with league and insurer sign-off.

*Referee capture.* In spectator mode, officials can wear cameras only in sanctioned pilots. IFAB's grassroots body-cam trial is limited to approved competitions ([IFAB](https://downloads.theifab.com/downloads/body-cameras-trial-protocol?l=en)). Washington's WIAA allowed body cams to deter abuse ([Coach & AD](https://coachad.com/?p=41664)). In both cases the footage serves incident review, not a stats library. The realistic primary wearer is a sideline parent or coach.

**The product design** follows from these constraints. Identify players primarily by jersey number, team color and roster, with human confirmation. Offer face recognition only as the opt-in, enrolled-only second path described above. Be explicit in public messaging that it never identifies strangers, given the Harvard I-XRAY doxxing demo and press reports of Meta's own "Name Tag" plans ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/someone-put-facial-recognition-tech-onto-metas-smart-glasses-to-instantly-dox-strangers); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/13/meta-facial-recognition-smart-glasses/)). Onboard at the organization level so that registration consents flow in, and give guardians tiered consent: appear, be named, be shared beyond the team. Keep libraries private to the team by default and add a per-player "do not identify" flag. Add a per-game rights-restricted flag for postseason and tournament games, set retention limits and run a takedown process. Exclude ad SDKs. None of this has been reviewed by counsel. The open questions are the scope of Meta's exception for enrolled-only face matching, ephemeral face detection for blurring, audio-recording consent laws and COPPA status for a parent-run app.

## Veo belongs in scope as a soccer MP4 import, not an integration

Veo recordings are worth accepting. The case for building a dedicated integration is weak, and a generic import path covers almost all of the value.

**What Veo produces.** Veo Cam 3 stitches two synchronized lenses into a 180-degree panorama from a mast up to 7.4 m high, and crops a 1080p AI follow-cam from it ([Veo Cam 3](https://www.veo.com/en-us/product/veo-cam-3)). The camera costs $999 plus a required subscription of roughly $84-$250 a month ([Veo US shop](https://us.shop.veo.co/)).

**Overlap with our product.** For soccer, Analytics 2 already does most of what a third party would build from this footage: event detection, shirt-number player ID with jersey "claiming", physical stats, heat and shot maps, Player Spotlight reels and an LLM "Coach Assist" ([Veo update](https://updates.veo.co/announcements/ann_93FN3qsJTPLfd)). Rebuilding those team-level stats from Veo video adds little.

**Export limits.** The only documented export is a single-match follow-cam MP4. "You can only download the follow-cam view of a match", the panorama is not downloadable, and bulk download is not possible ([Veo support](https://support.veo.com/hc/en-us/articles/4451733961361); [Cutsio](https://cutsio.com/blog/how-to-download-veo-recordings.md)). No CSV, JSON or XML export of events or stats was found. Users' request for XML export to Sportscode and Nacsport is unconfirmed ([Veo Ideas](https://veo.nolt.io/997)). Veo's API is a limited first version with no public documentation. An integrator calls its "primary design" one for "ease of sharing rather than deep platform integration" ([Icareus](https://icareus.com/how-to-integrate-spiideo-and-veo-videos-into-a-sports-streaming-platform/)).

**Terms of service.** Veo's terms, effective 29 May 2026, say customers own their uploaded content. Veo nevertheless takes a sublicensable license that includes AI training, and owns the "insights and models" derived from that content ([Veo Terms](https://www.veo.com/terms)). That likely explains why there is no stats export. Framesports connects users' Veo accounts with their own credentials and calls it its "most popular integration" ([Framesports](https://docs.framesports.ai/games/video-sources)). Veo's terms contain no explicit anti-scraping clause, but their restrictions on downloading Veo "Material" and on interfering with the service make credential scraping a gray area.

**Recommendation.** Veo is in scope as a phase-two, soccer-first input through a generic "import fixed-camera MP4" path that also serves XbotGo, Trace and phone recordings. The unique value is fusion. Veo supplies the wide, stable, full-game backbone that glasses cannot record on one battery. The glasses supply close-range jersey reads, the wearer's voice tags, and the "my player" moments that Veo's far-touchline OCR misses. The two can be synced by cross-correlating whistle audio or by aligning kickoff visually, since Veo publishes no timecode.

**Other sports and fixed cameras.** Veo also films American football and baseball, without any AI analytics, so its MP4s are simply fixed-camera footage our pipeline would analyze from scratch. If programmatic fixed-camera ingestion becomes a requirement, Pixellot is the better-documented target. It has a Partner API with staging and production environments and webhooks, and it is common in US high schools ([Pixellot API](https://docs.pixellot.tv/portal/en/kb/articles/pixellot-marketplace-api-documentation)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the product. A fully automatic glasses pipeline would lose to Veo and Hudl on their own ground. The defensible design is a system where the wearer's attention and voice do the hard parts. Where the parent looks solves identity without beacons. What the coach says solves attribution of tackles, assists and ball/strike calls, which vision cannot settle. Computer vision and VLMs then segment, timestamp, confirm and clip. The same human-seeding logic shapes face recognition. Labeling a few shots per enrolled person turns a stranger-identification risk into a closed-set, consented match against a known roster. That keeps it defensible to Meta, to regulators and to a press that associates glasses face ID with doxxing. It also means the Meta exception and per-person consent flow are product-critical work for pickup play, not legal afterthoughts.

The planned sport order deserves a second look. Football has the thinnest open tooling, the hardest identity problem (helmets, no jersey-OCR benchmarks) and an incumbent selling human breakdown at up to $1,700 a season. It works as a first sport only if v1 is voice-assisted play logging plus per-player highlights, not automated all-22 breakdown. Baseball is the sleeper. It has the most structured event grammar (Retrosheet), a compliance-grade stat that coaches must track (pitch counts), no video auto-scorer on the market, and a dugout-coach POV that is plausible under equipment rules. Two platform facts will gate any launch more than model quality: whether a battery-limited 720p stream can survive a full game with case swaps, and whether Meta opens public DAT distribution beyond 100-tester release channels. Both should be checked early, on real Gen 3 and Vanguard hardware.
